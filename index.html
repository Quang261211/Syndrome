01 / The big picture

What is a tense?

A tense helps us show when an action happens and, in many cases, whether it is simple, ongoing or completed.

PAST

Actions or situations before now.

yesterday · last year
PRESENT

Habits, facts, current actions and experiences connected to now.

usually · now · since
FUTURE

Predictions, plans and actions expected later.

tomorrow · next week
Learning tip: identify the time first, then decide whether to use a simple, continuous, perfect or perfect continuous form.

✓

03 / Present tenses
01

Present Simple

Use it for habits, routines, facts and permanent situations.

Form

PositiveS + V(s/es) + O
NegativeS + do/does not + V
QuestionDo/Does + S + V?

With be: am / is / are. Add -s/-es for he, she and it.

When & how?

• Daily routines and repeated actions
• General truths and facts
• Timetables and schedules

She studies English every evening.
alwaysusuallyoftenevery day
03 / Present tenses
02

Present Continuous

Use it for actions happening now or temporary situations around the present.

Form

PositiveS + am/is/are + V-ing
NegativeS + am/is/are not + V-ing
QuestionAm/Is/Are + S + V-ing?

When & how?

• Actions happening at the moment
• Temporary situations
• Changing or developing situations
• Arranged future plans

Look! The children are playing outside.
nowright nowat the momentcurrently
Some state verbs (e.g. know, believe, belong) are not normally used in continuous forms.
03 / Present tenses
03

Present Perfect

Connects a past action or experience to the present. The exact past time is usually not stated.

Form

PositiveS + have/has + V3
NegativeS + have/has not + V3
QuestionHave/Has + S + V3?

When & how?

• Life experiences
• Recently completed actions with present results
• Situations that started in the past and continue now

I have finished my homework.
alreadyyeteverneversince / for
03 / Present tenses
04

Present Perfect Continuous

Emphasises the duration or ongoing process of an action that began in the past.

Form

PositiveS + have/has been + V-ing
NegativeS + have/has not been + V-ing
QuestionHave/Has + S + been + V-ing?

When & how?

• An activity continuing up to now
• A recent activity with a visible result
• Focus on duration rather than completion

She has been studying for three hours.
forsinceall daylatelyhow long
04 / Past tenses
05

Past Simple

Describes an action or situation that started and finished in the past.

Form

PositiveS + V2 / V-ed
NegativeS + did not + V
QuestionDid + S + V?

With be: was / were.

When & how?

• Finished past events
• A sequence of past actions
• Past habits or states

We visited Da Lat last summer.
yesterdaylast weekagoin 2020
04 / Past tenses
06

Past Continuous

Describes an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past.

Form

PositiveS + was/were + V-ing
NegativeS + was/were not + V-ing
QuestionWas/Were + S + V-ing?

When & how?

• An action in progress at a past time
• A longer action interrupted by a shorter one
• Two actions happening simultaneously

I was reading when the phone rang.
whilewhenat 8 p.m. yesterday
04 / Past tenses
07

Past Perfect

Shows that one past action happened before another past action or time.

Form

PositiveS + had + V3
NegativeS + had not + V3
QuestionHad + S + V3?

When & how?

• The earlier of two past actions
• Something completed before a past deadline

The train had left before we arrived.
beforeafterby the timealready
04 / Past tenses
08

Past Perfect Continuous

Emphasises how long an activity had continued before a point or event in the past.

Form

PositiveS + had been + V-ing
NegativeS + had not been + V-ing
QuestionHad + S + been + V-ing?

When & how?

• Duration before another past event
• A past result explained by an ongoing activity

He was tired because he had been running.
forsincebeforeuntil thenby the time
05 / Future forms
09

Future Simple

Uses will for predictions, promises, offers and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

Form

PositiveS + will + V
NegativeS + will not (won't) + V
QuestionWill + S + V?

When & how?

• Spontaneous decisions
• Predictions and opinions
• Promises, offers and requests

I think it will rain tomorrow.
tomorrownext weekI thinkprobably
05 / Future forms
10

Near Future

Uses be going to for prior intentions and predictions supported by present evidence.

Form

PositiveS + am/is/are going to + V
NegativeS + am/is/are not going to + V
QuestionAm/Is/Are + S + going to + V?

When & how?

• Plans or intentions decided earlier
• Predictions based on what we can see now

Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.
tonighttomorrownext monthlook!
“Near Future” is a common teaching label for the be going to construction; it is often taught alongside the 12 core tense forms.
05 / Future forms
11

Future Continuous

Describes an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

Form

PositiveS + will be + V-ing
NegativeS + will not be + V-ing
QuestionWill + S + be + V-ing?

When & how?

• An action in progress at a future time
• Expected activities as part of a future routine

At 9 tomorrow, I will be taking an exam.
this time tomorrowat 9 p.m.next week
05 / Future forms
12

Future Perfect

Describes an action that will be completed before a specific future time or event.

Form

PositiveS + will have + V3
NegativeS + will not have + V3
QuestionWill + S + have + V3?

When & how?

• Completion before a future deadline
• Looking back from a point in the future

By Friday, she will have finished the project.
by Fridayby thenby the timebefore
05 / Future forms
13

Future Perfect Continuous

Emphasises the duration of an activity up to a particular point in the future.

Form

PositiveS + will have been + V-ing
NegativeS + will not have been + V-ing
QuestionWill + S + have been + V-ing?

When & how?

• Duration continuing up to a future reference point
• Focus on how long an activity will have lasted

By June, I will have been learning English for two years.
forby thenby next yearby the time
06 / Review

Quick comparison

Notice how the verb pattern changes the meaning.

FormExampleMain idea
SimpleI study.Habit / fact
ContinuousI am studying.In progress now
PerfectI have studied.Past connected to now
Perfect ContinuousI have been studying.Duration up to now
Past PerfectI had studied.Earlier past action
Future PerfectI will have studied.Complete by a future time
Remember: signal words are clues, not automatic answers. Always read the whole sentence and understand its meaning.
07 / Check your understanding

Mini practice

Choose the correct verb form for each sentence, then reveal the answers.

01

She usually ______ (walk) to school.

walks / is walking

02

Look! They ______ (play) football.

are playing / play

03

I ______ (live) here since 2022.

have lived / lived

04

When I arrived, the film ______ (start).

had started / has started

05

This time tomorrow, we ______ (travel).

will be travelling / travel

06

By next May, he ______ (work) here for ten years.

will have been working / works

Answers: 1. walks · 2. are playing · 3. have lived · 4. had started · 5. will be travelling · 6. will have been working
